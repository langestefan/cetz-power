The three canonical ways a low-voltage service connection taps a street
cable, each fed from the same MS/LS substation chain:

- **A.1a** — a branch joint (_aftakmof_) on an **LS-hoofdkabel**;
- **A.1b** — the auxiliary cores (_hulpaders_) of an **LS-combikabel**,
  shown "zoomed" out of the dashed cable outline;
- **A.1c** — an **OV** public-lighting cable with a branch drop
  (_aftaksysteem_) and a loop-through (_rijgsysteem_), plus the
  LS-hoofdkabel continuing as a dashed run.

Every variant carries its cable cross-section on the right, and the
red-lettered chain (_Knip → Verbinding → Overdrachtspunt_) marks where
the service is cut in, jointed and handed over.

## How it's built

- One `station(tag, y)` helper draws each MS → transformer → LS chain;
  the small white squares are **one-node**
  [`breaker`](/symbols/protection/breaker/)s (`fill: white`, so they
  mask the run like the book's switchgear boxes), and every joint /
  service box is a closed [`junction`](/symbols/grid/junction/).
- The service drops share a `drop(tag, x, ...)` helper — cable, transfer
  point square, service-box dot — and the _rijgsysteem_ is just a
  U-shaped [`wire`](/symbols/grid/wire/) between two junctions on the OV
  run with the same drop hanging off its far corner.
- The **cross-sections are raw CeTZ**: an `xsec(...)` closure draws the
  sheath circle and four conductor circles (plus the combikabel's four
  small hulpader cores in between). The neutral's yellow/blue hatching
  is a Typst [`tiling`](https://typst.app/docs/reference/visualize/tiling/)
  fill — a 6 pt tile of yellow with a diagonal blue stripe — passed
  straight to `cetz.draw.circle(fill: ..)`.
- The dashed combikabel outline is three plain dashed `cetz.draw.line`s
  — open on the right, its top and bottom edges running straight into
  the cross-section sheath, whose white fill masks the overshoot (the
  conductors continue into it the same way).
