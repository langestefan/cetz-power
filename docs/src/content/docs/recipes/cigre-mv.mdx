The modified CIGRE medium-voltage benchmark on one canvas: an
[external grid](/symbols/grid/external-grid/) feeds the 110 kV **bus 0**,
which splits onto two **parallel 110/20 kV transformers** — each with an
HV-side [switch](/symbols/protection/switch/). **Feeder 1** (nodes 1–11,
with **LV-feeder 1** at node 6) and **Feeder 2** (nodes 12–14, with
**LV-feeder 2** at node 14) hang off them, and the two feeders are tied
through switch **S1** (2.0 km). Every line segment carries its length and
the three-stroke phase-conductor mark; the flexible devices — loads and
rooftop PV — are drawn in blue.

## How it's built

- Every numbered point in the figure is a **real busbar**, so each is a short
  horizontal [`bus`](/symbols/grid/bus/) drawn by a `bbar(name, x, y, hl)`
  closure. The through-lines tap each bar at its centre while loads, PV and
  the bus number hang off offset points addressed with
  [`bus-frac`](/symbols/grid/bus/) (e.g. `bus-frac("b1", 0.25)`) or the
  `start`/`end` anchors — that's what keeps a load, a PV and a passing feeder
  from all landing on the same spot. Bus 3 is a wide bar because it branches
  (down to Bus 7, left to Bus 4); the rest are narrow.
- `link(a, b, lbl, lside, ang)` joins two bus points with a
  [`wire`](/symbols/grid/wire/), stamps the **three diagonal phase ticks** at
  35 % along it, and drops the length label. The ticks are a tiny CeTZ group —
  `set-origin` to the tick point, `rotate` to the segment angle (`ang`), then
  three short parallel strokes — so they sit square across the line whatever
  its direction.
- `bload(...)` is a blue [`load`](/symbols/loads/load/) and `bpv(...)` a blue
  [`pv-panel`](/symbols/generation/pv-panel/) — both reuse the stock symbols,
  only the colour changes.
- The two **110/20 kV** banks are a [`switch`](/symbols/protection/switch/)
  over a [`transformer`](/symbols/grid/transformer/), drawn in a `for` loop
  over the two feeder columns; the **LV-feeder** buses (6 and 14) drop through
  a small `transformer` to a blue load. The **S1/S2/S3** disconnectors are
  stock `switch`es spliced straight between bus anchors, and the two dashed
  **feeder boundaries** are background `rect`s.
