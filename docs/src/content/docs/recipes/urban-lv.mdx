The urban low-voltage benchmark grid (TU/e thesis on reactive power
management and flexibility aggregation, fig. 4.9): a **150 MVA, 10 kV**
external grid feeds bus 1, an OLTC **10/0.4 kV** transformer steps down
onto bus 2, and three radial 400 V feeders serve 34 buses — buses 3–12
with the 6–9 sub-branch, 13–22 with the 17–19 and 28/29 spurs, and 23–34.
Five normally-open switches **S1–S5** tie the feeder ends together along
the right edge, so the network can be reconfigured into rings. Firm loads
are black arrows, **flexible loads are blue**, and eight PV panels hang
off their buses as chevron boxes.

## How it's built

The layout was digitised from the published figure, so every coordinate
is an image pixel passed through one mapping:

```typst
let s = 0.016                        // image-pixel → cm (compression knob)
let P(x, y) = (x * s, (H - y) * s)   // flip y (image is y-down)
```

- Every one of the 34 nodes is a real vertical [`bus`](/symbols/grid/bus/)
  tick (`nbus`), long enough that feeders cross its **interior** and
  devices tap its lower **body** — never a tip. Double-row junction buses
  (12, 19, 22, 29) simply let both conductors cross the same tick.
- Loads leave their tick with an L-bend (`load(..., elbow: ..)`) — across,
  then arrow down; flexible ones just override `fill`/`stroke` with the
  figure's cyan. PV panels do the same via `pv-panel(..., elbow: ..)`.
- Devices are drawn **before** their buses, so the thick bars paint over
  the lead roots — a blue lead would otherwise show its connection point
  sitting on top of the bar.
- Each feeder hop is one `hs(x1, x2, y, [.. m])` call: a
  [`wire`](/symbols/grid/wire/) plus a `note` at the midpoint (wires can't
  carry labels); vertical runs get rotated length labels.
- The five tie [`switch`](/symbols/protection/switch/)es sit on short
  verticals in a shared right-edge column, each spanning the same 34 px
  gap, with the S-branch lengths (30/20/10/5/5 m) labelled on the bus
  side, as in the original figure.
