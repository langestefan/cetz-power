The urban low-voltage benchmark grid (TU/e thesis on reactive power
management and flexibility aggregation, fig. 4.9): a **150 MVA, 10 kV**
external grid feeds bus 1, an OLTC **10/0.4 kV** transformer steps down
onto bus 2, and three radial 400 V feeders serve 34 buses — buses 3–12
with the 6–9 sub-branch, 13–22 with the 17–19 and 28/29 spurs, and 23–34.
Five normally-open switches **S1–S5** tie the feeder ends together along
the right edge, so the network can be reconfigured into rings. Firm loads
are black arrows, **flexible loads are blue**, and eight PV panels hang
off their buses as chevron boxes.

## How it's built

The drawing is **data-driven**: each feeder is a table of stations
`(segment length [m], bus nr, device)`, and `place()` turns cable
lengths into bus positions at a fixed pitch `k` (cm per metre) — no
hand-placed coordinates per bus:

```typst
let A = place(x2 + 0.4, (
  (none, 3, "l"), (20, 4, "f"), (30, 5, "pv"),
  (25, 10, "f"), (30, 11, "l"), (25, 12, "pv"),
))
run(ya, x2 + 0.4, A)
```

- `run()` draws a whole feeder: labelled wire hops, then the devices
  (`"l"` firm load, `"f"` blue flexible load, `"pv"` panel), then the
  numbered [`bus`](/symbols/grid/bus/) ticks — devices first so the bars
  paint over the lead roots.
- Branches reference computed positions via `xof(run, bus)`: the
  `riser()` helper leaves a tick's body sideways by one shared `dbr`
  offset and climbs to the sub-feeder's row; the `tie()` helper hangs
  each normally-open [`switch`](/symbols/protection/switch/) S1–S5 off a
  run's end and lands it in the destination tick's interior.
- Feeders cross every tick's **interior**, devices tap the lower body
  with an L-bend (`elbow:`), and all spacing comes from ~15 named
  constants (`lead`, `dbr`, `dj`, `tail`, row heights, …), so the whole
  figure compresses or stretches by editing one number.
