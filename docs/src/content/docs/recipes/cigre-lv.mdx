The full modified CIGRE low-voltage benchmark on one canvas: an external
grid feeds an MV bus (switch **S2**, 11 kV) and an **11/0.4 kV, 250 kVA**
transformer onto the 400 V **LV bus**, which fans out into three radial
feeders — **residential** (R1–R13), **industrial** (a single 100 m run to
I1), and **commercial** (C1–C14). Every segment carries its length, and
loads hang off the nodes as direction arrows.

## How it's built

It's a lot of repetition, so the whole diagram runs on six tiny closures
defined at the top of the snippet:

- `dot(p)` — a filled node circle; `nl(p, c, s)` — a node label
  ([`note`](/helpers/feeder/) at a side); `dl(a, b, c, s)` — a segment-length
  label at the midpoint (`(a, 50%, b)` lerp).
- `seg(a, b)` — a [`wire`](/symbols/grid/wire/); `ld(name, p, ang)` — a
  directional [`load`](/symbols/loads/load/) arrow (`ang` in degrees: `0`
  down, `90` right, `-90` left), with `fill: none` for the hollow ▷ / ◁
  loads. `drop(...)` taps a node circle a distance below its parent and
  hangs a load off it — that's the C9/C10/C13/C14/R12 pattern in one line.
- The three feeders are then just node coordinates plus loops: the
  residential and commercial main lines are `range`-mapped
  (`R = i => (rx, by - i*rs)`), and branches are placed relative to them.
  Rooftop PV at R8/R9 is the [`pv-panel`](/symbols/generation/pv-panel/) symbol.
- The top chain is built explicitly — [`external-grid`](/symbols/grid/external-grid/),
  a [`switch`](/symbols/protection/switch/) (S2) above a
  [`bus`](/symbols/grid/bus/) (the MV bus), and a
  [`transformer`](/symbols/grid/transformer/) down to the LV `bus`.

Because it's all coordinates and closures, re-spacing a feeder or adding a
node is a one-line change.
