The classic Dutch distribution-network overview (Phase to Phase, *Netten
voor distributie van elektriciteit*, fig. 2.8): a **150 kV** rail feeds
two 150/10 kV transformers onto a double **10 kV** rail in the
substation, from which a **hoofdring** (main ring) leaves one rail
section, runs across the top and returns along the bottom. A **subring**
nests inside it and an **uitloper** (spur) hangs off the bottom row.

The ring is operated *open*: the two **netopeningen** near the top-right
node are open [`switch`](/symbols/protection/switch/) blades, and the
hollow circles on the rails are open
[`junction`](/symbols/grid/junction/) connection points (filled = closed).
The **×** marks are the switchgear positions —
[`breaker(kind: "cross")`](/symbols/protection/breaker/) — at the
transformer bays, the rail coupling and every feeder head.

## How it's built

- The layout is digitised from the figure through one pixel map,
  `P(x, y) = (x*s, (H - y)*s)` — every coordinate in the source is an
  image pixel.
- Rails are [`bus`](/symbols/grid/bus/)es (the 150 kV one just overrides
  `stroke: 1.8pt + red`); the HV winding of each transformer is coloured
  with `primary-stroke: 0.8pt + red`.
- All connection points are `junction`s placed **after** the conductors:
  a closed one is a filled dot on the line, and an open one paints its
  interior white, masking the wire so the network visibly splits there —
  that is exactly how the substation's spare rail connections and the
  ring's open points read.
- The × marks are one-node `breaker(kind: "cross")` calls dropped
  straight onto the runs — no need to split a wire into two segments per
  mark.
- The two netopeningen are small open `switch`es spanning exactly their
  pin gap, with `pivot-radius: 0` for the book's bare-blade look; the
  hoofdring one flips its blade below the line with `open-angle: -30deg`.
