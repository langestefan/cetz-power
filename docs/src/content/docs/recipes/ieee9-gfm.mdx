The **IEEE nine-bus** test system in its converter-dominated form (as
used for Monte-Carlo validation of small-signal stability conditions):
the three machine buses 1–3 carry **grid-forming converters GFM 1–3**
behind their step-up transformers, two **grid-following converter
loads** tie in through their own transformers (GFL load 1 at bus 5,
GFL load 2 at bus 8), a **synchronous generator SG** shares bus 8, and
bus 6 carries a constant-power load.

## How it's built

- **Converter blocks are the stock symbol.** Every GFM / GFL unit is a
  [`converter`](/symbols/electrical/converter/) box — black lines with
  a light-blue (GFM) or grey (GFL) interior — wired to its bus or
  transformer through a compass anchor, no floating boxes. Each box
  faces its **AC (≈) triangle toward the grid connection**: boxes left
  of their connection use `kind: "dc-ac"`, the rest keep the default
  `"ac-dc"`.
- **Mirror symmetry about bus 8 / bus 4.** Buses 7 and 9, buses 5 and
  6, and the two funnel diagonals into bus 4 are exact reflections:
  equal elbow stubs off the rail buses, equal perpendicular stubs at
  both ends of each diagonal.
- **Interior taps everywhere.** The drops leave the vertical rail
  buses from their _body_ (a short horizontal stub, then an elbow
  down), never from a bar tip; bus 5 and bus 6 carry their two
  connections at equal spacing and equal edge margins.
- **Two feeders off bus 8.** The GFL-load-2 and SG branches each leave
  the bottom of bus 8 through their own double-L route (a short stub
  out of the bar body, down, then across into the transformer lead),
  with the two transformer bodies equidistant from the bus-8 axis.
- **The SG is a stock symbol too** — a
  [`voltagesource`](/symbols/electrical/voltagesource/) with
  `kind: "sin"`, the circle-with-sine generator.
